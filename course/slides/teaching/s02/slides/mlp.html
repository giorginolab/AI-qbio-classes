Neural networks

Layers of weighted sums and simple bends, tuned by gradient descent.

A multilayer perceptron (MLP) passes the input through layers of units. Each unit computes a weighted sum of its inputs and applies a fixed non-linear function. With ReLU, max(0, z), each unit adds a bend to the curve.

Training starts from random weights and changes them in small steps that reduce the error: gradient descent. The notebook’s network has two layers of 64 units, about 4 400 weights.

A ReLU network is piecewise linear, so far from the data it continues as a straight line.

The last point is the key to Part 2. Other activation functions behave differently far from the data: a tanh network, for example, saturates and goes flat. Either way, the behaviour outside the data comes from the architecture, not from the data.
