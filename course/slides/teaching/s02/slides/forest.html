Trees and random forests

A tree asks yes-or-no questions; a forest averages many trees.

A decision tree splits the data with questions such as ‘is the mass below some threshold?’ and predicts the mean of the training points that end in the same leaf. As a function of mass, it is a staircase.

One deep tree memorises its training set. A random forest grows hundreds of trees, each on a bootstrap sample and with random choices, and averages them: the average is far more stable than any single tree. Like kNN, every prediction is an average of training values.

The notebook uses 200 trees. Forests are strong default models for tables of features, which is why they appear in every session of the course.
