Bias and variance

Prediction error comes from wrong assumptions and from sensitivity to the sample.

Bias: a model too rigid for the true relation, such as a straight line through a curve, is wrong in the same way whatever sample it is given.

Variance: a model flexible enough to follow the noise changes a lot from one sample to the next.

More flexibility lowers bias and raises variance. Cross-validation helps find a good balance inside the data. It says nothing about what happens outside them.

Link to session 1: the metabolic law has a small bias, because the true relation is slightly bent (slopes 0.60, 0.71 and 0.82 for small, medium and large mammals). The flexible models can follow the bend but pay with more variance. At n = 422 on one feature, the two effects roughly cancel, which is why all four scores are similar.
