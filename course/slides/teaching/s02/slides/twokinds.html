Two kinds of model

A law fixes the shape in advance. A flexible model learns the shape from the data.

A parametric law such as B = a · M^b has a form chosen from theory or from a plot; the data only set a few numbers, here two. The form carries knowledge, and the risk is that the form is wrong.

A flexible model assumes little more than ‘similar inputs give similar outputs’. Given enough data it can follow almost any curve, but it knows nothing beyond the data it was shown.

The words parametric and nonparametric are used loosely: a neural network has thousands of parameters, yet its shape is not fixed in advance. What matters for this session is how strongly the form is constrained before the model sees any data. The whole session is one contrast between these two families.
