Cross-validation

Score a model on data it did not see during training.

Training error rewards memorisation, so it cannot compare a two-parameter law with a model of thousands of weights. In k-fold cross-validation the data are split into k groups; each group is predicted by a model trained on the others.

R² = 1 − (squared error of the model) / (squared error of always predicting the mean). 1 is perfect, 0 is no better than the mean, negative is worse.

Cross-validation estimates performance on new data drawn like the training data. Keep that condition in mind.

The notebook uses 5 folds and cross_val_score, which returns one R² per fold; the spread across folds tells how much of a difference between models is noise. The condition in the last line is the thread of Parts 2 and 3: both break it, in two different ways.
