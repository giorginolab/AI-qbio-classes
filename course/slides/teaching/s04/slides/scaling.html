Scaling

Bigger models are often better, until they are not

For language models, larger networks trained on more data usually give lower error on the pretraining task, and the gain often follows a smooth curve: a scaling law, like the power laws of session 1.

A better pretraining score does not guarantee a better representation for your task. Returns can diminish, or stop, when the task, the data or the downstream model becomes the bottleneck.

ESM-2 exists in several sizes; the data file contains embeddings of the same enzymes from 8M, 35M and 150M parameter models.
