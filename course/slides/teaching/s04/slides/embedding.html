Embeddings

An embedding is a protein as a point in a learned space

Inside the network every residue becomes a vector that describes it in its context: the same lysine gets different vectors in different proteins.

To get one vector per protein we average the residue vectors along the sequence (mean pooling). The result, 320 numbers for the small ESM-2, is the protein’s embedding.

Proteins that the network finds similar end up close together in this space.

Mean pooling is the simplest choice. Alternatives exist (a special start token, attention-weighted pooling) but averaging works surprisingly well for classification.
