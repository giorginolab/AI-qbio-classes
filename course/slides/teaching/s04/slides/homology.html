Relatives in the data

Proteins have relatives, and relatives share answers

Members of a protein family descend from a common ancestor: they share a fold and often a function.

With a random split most test enzymes have a relative in the training set. A model can then score well by recognising the family rather than learning anything about the chemistry.

The test set then measures “find the relative”, not “predict function for a new protein”.

Same issue as related species in session 2, and as near-duplicate molecules in QSAR. Whenever samples are not independent, a random split is optimistic.
