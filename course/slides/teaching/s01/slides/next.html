Is “quarter-power scaling” a law or a rule of thumb?

Try (homework, Part 7): fit the exponents of gestation time, age at maturity, litter size and birth mass against adult mass. Which are close to 1/4? Pick one trait, list the largest residuals, and explain them.

Read: Kleiber 1932 on metabolic scaling; West, Brown and Enquist 1997 on the 3/4 theory; Kolokotrones et al. 2010 on curvature; Tacutu et al. 2018 for the AnAge database.

Notebook solution: gestation and maturity scale near 0.21, longevity 0.16, all with R² around 0.5; birth mass 0.91; litter size −0.10. For gestation residuals, look at marsupials.
