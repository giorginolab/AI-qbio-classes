Logarithms

Biology is multiplicative, so measure it on a log scale

Body masses in our table run from a 2.4 g shrew to a 3.7 t elephant: six orders of magnitude. Growth, metabolism and many measurements vary by factors, not by fixed amounts.

A logarithm turns factors into distances: on a log10 axis every ×10 takes the same space. Equal steps then mean equal ratios, and a mouse and an elephant can sit on the same plot.

Quick check: log10(1000) = 3, log10(0.01) = −2. A difference of 1 in log10 is a factor 10; a difference of 0.3 is a factor of about 2. Other quantities that need log scales: gene expression, concentrations (pH, LC50), population sizes, genome sizes (session 2).
