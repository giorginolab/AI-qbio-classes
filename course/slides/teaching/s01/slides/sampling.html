Sampling variability

A fitted number would change with another sample

Our 422 species are one sample of the mammals that could have been measured. Another sample would give a slightly different slope.

The question “how precisely do we know b?” is about this variability. It matters here because two theories predict different exponents, 2/3 (surface heat loss) and 3/4 (branching networks, West, Brown and Enquist 1997), and our 0.71 lies between them.

Classical formulas give a standard error for the slope under assumptions (independent errors, constant variance). The bootstrap gets the same kind of answer with fewer assumptions and works for any statistic.
