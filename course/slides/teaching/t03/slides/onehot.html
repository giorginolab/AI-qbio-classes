Representation · one-hot encoding

One-hot encoding turns a sequence into numbers without inventing an order among amino acids

Each residue becomes a vector of 20 zeros with a single 1 in the column of its amino acid. A 50-residue N-terminus becomes a 50 × 20 matrix; the whole dataset a 3-dimensional array (proteins × positions × amino acids), a tensor.

Why not code A = 1, C = 2, …? Because a model would read C as “between” A and D. One-hot makes every amino acid equally different from every other, and lets the model learn which differences matter.

Alternatives: physico-chemical descriptors per residue (hydropathy, charge, volume), rows of a substitution matrix such as BLOSUM, or learned embeddings, which is what protein language models produce (session 4). One-hot keeps the position: the row index is the position in the sequence.
