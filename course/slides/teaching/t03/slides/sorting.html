Biology · protein sorting

Many proteins carry an address label at their N-terminus

A eukaryotic cell makes most proteins in the cytosol, yet many must end up in the endoplasmic reticulum, the membrane, outside the cell or in mitochondria. The destination is often written in the first tens of residues.

Signal peptide (SP), about 15–35 residues: sends the protein into the secretory pathway. Found in all kingdoms of life.

Mitochondrial transit peptide (mTP), about 20–60 residues: imports the protein into mitochondria. Only in eukaryotes.

Both signals are cut off once the protein has arrived (by signal peptidase and by the mitochondrial processing peptidase). Proteins with no N-terminal signal stay in the cytosol or are sent to the nucleus or other compartments by other signals. Ask: why are there no mTPs in E. coli or B. subtilis? Bacteria have no mitochondria; mitochondria descend from bacteria.
