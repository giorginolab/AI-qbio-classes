Capacity and overfitting

More parameters fit the training data better, not necessarily new data

A model’s capacity is how many different shapes it can take. Low capacity misses real structure (underfitting). High capacity can also follow the noise of the particular sample (overfitting).

The symptom is a gap: the training score keeps improving while the score on unseen data stalls or gets worse. That gap, not the training score, tells you how the model will behave.

Classical name: the bias-variance trade-off. Bias is the error from too rigid assumptions, variance is the sensitivity to which sample you happened to draw. Flexible models have low bias and high variance.
