Inductive bias

A neural network is not automatically the best model

0.58

MLP, 5-fold CV R²

0.60

random forest

Every model carries assumptions about what the answer looks like: its inductive bias. Trees assume the answer changes in steps along single descriptors; a network assumes smooth piecewise-linear surfaces.

On a small table of a few hand-made descriptors, trees are hard to beat. Networks pay off with large data and raw, structured inputs such as sequences and images, where they can learn their own features.

Exercise A.1: MLP R² 0.576 against random forest 0.604 with the same 5-fold cross-validation. The fish table has 908 chemicals and 6 descriptors. The point for students: choosing a model is choosing assumptions, and the right ones depend on the data.
