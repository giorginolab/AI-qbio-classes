Question

Bats and primates sit above the line, opossums and shrews below. Why?

And is the human value comparable with the others?

A common explanation is the risk of being eaten. Animals that escape predators (flying, living in trees, being clever or social) can benefit from investing in a durable body; for a shrew or an opossum, which rarely survives two years in the wild, it does not pay. The fitted line said nothing of this, but its residuals pointed at the question. Caveat: maximum longevity is a record. For humans it is taken over billions of people with birth certificates, for most species over a few zoo animals; a record grows with the number of individuals observed.
