The valley is long, narrow and tilted. Why?

Gradient descent drops in fast, then crawls along it. Try lr = 0.15: what happens?

Intercept and slope trade off: all MLOGP values are around 2, so a steeper slope is almost compensated by a lower intercept. Big steps across steep walls, tiny steps along the flat floor: most of the 300 steps are spent creeping. With lr = 0.15 the steps overshoot wall to wall and the loss explodes; any learning rate above about 0.14 diverges. Centring MLOGP makes the valley round: one reason we standardise inputs of neural networks. Test loss follows training loss down: a straight line has too few parameters to overfit.
